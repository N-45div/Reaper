How it works

From upload to proof, without you in the middle

One upload starts a months-long autonomous pipeline. You appear exactly once — to approve the notice before it goes out.

Read & verify

Gemini quotes the renewal clause verbatim; an independent deterministic date engine re-derives the notice deadline from the raw text. Any disagreement blocks scheduling.

the gate

Sleep on it

The obligation is scheduled and the agent goes durably to sleep — for weeks if needed. State lives in the database, not in a process.

zero token burn

Notice, approved by you

Inside the notice window it wakes, drafts the formal non-renewal notice and pauses for your approval. The pause survives restarts. Then it sends, and hash-stamps the delivery receipt.

one human approval

Trust, then verify

When the next invoice lands, a deterministic check confirms billing stopped. If the vendor billed anyway, Reaper opens the dispute — attaching its own timestamped receipt as evidence.

the closed loop
Why it's different

An AI that refuses to trust itself

A hallucinated date must never become a missed deadline. So every deadline is derived twice — once by the model, once by plain calendar math — and nothing is scheduled unless they agree.

⏸

Pauses that survive anything

Waiting for your approval, the agent can be killed, redeployed or restarted — and resumes exactly where it paused. Durability is the architecture, not a feature flag.

⛓

Evidence that fights back

Every action lands in a SHA-256 hash-chained ledger. When a vendor bills after cancelling, the dispute ships with your delivery receipt — tamper-evident, timestamped.

✓

Outcomes, not alerts

Reminders get ignored. Reaper closes the loop: notice sent, billing verified stopped, or dispute filed. The chore is finished, not forwarded to you.
